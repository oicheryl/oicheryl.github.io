Three speakers from Testkube, MongoDB, and Cisco joined us at the October Cloud Native London meetup at the Just Eat office, as well as via the YouTube/LinkedIn livestreams. 

We are immensely grateful to [Testkube](https://testkube.io/), [MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/), [cloudsmith](https://cloudsmith.com), and [TechShack](https://www.tech-shack.co/) for their generous support and sponsorship. 

### Overview
Kicking off our evening was Ole Lensmar from Testkube discussing [Automating Quality in Delivery Pipelines](https://www.youtube.com/live/Z7PtjaKost4?si=a1Kv8HidEsmccyUN&t=698)(starting at 11:38). His takeaways explained that:
* Teams are increasingly looking to automate the entire SDLC with AI.	
* The biggest challenge with autonomous AI for quality is trust.	
* Automating quality in pipelines requires context beyond code and tests.

Following this was Ricardo Villanueva from MongoDB talking about [Fewer Moving Parts, Lower Cloud Costs: Rethinking the Cloud-Native Data Stack](https://www.youtube.com/live/Z7PtjaKost4?si=dApX9gXi5oLA-0Az&t=2370)(starting at 39:30). His takeaways are that:
* Every extra component has a “hidden bill”: infrastructure, integration work, monitoring, upgrades, incident response, and the risk of inconsistent data.
* Integrated data services can remove synchronization work. MongoDB Search combines the database, search engine, and sync mechanism in a managed platform.
* Architecture simplification can free teams to build product features. Helvetia reported that Atlas Search removed separate data-sync pipelines and reduced developer and operational overhead.

And wrapping up our evening, we had Hasith Kalpage from Cisco explaining how [AI Productivity Isn't a Model Problem, It's a Platform Problem — CAIPE.io's Story So Far…](https://www.youtube.com/live/Z7PtjaKost4?si=Sh-clXY7iQwSGDsZ&t=5538)(starting at 1:32:18). He explored how:
* CAIPE.io — Community AI Platform Engineering.
Every enterprise standing up agents hits the same wall: multi-channel deployment, identity and RBAC, tool integration, audit trails, context. None of it is differentiating — it's table stakes, and rebuilding it at every company is wasted effort. CAIPE is Apache 2.0, self-hosted, community-governed, and already proven in production. Let's build the foundation for AI productivity together.
* Platform engineering patterns transfer — agents are just a new kind of workload.
CAIPE grew out of Backstage-style developer platform thinking, and the core lessons carried over directly: self-service beats ticket queues, pluggable integrations (MCP) beat hard-coded ones, and identity and RBAC have to be designed in from day one, not bolted on when security asks. The novelty is the workload, not the discipline.
* Context is infrastructure, and it has to be shared.
The biggest unlock wasn't a better agent — it was giving agents and engineers the same view of the organisation: code, decisions, and discussions synthesised into something both can read. Without shared memory, every agent session starts from zero and every human re-explains the same context. With it, small teams operate well above their headcount.
